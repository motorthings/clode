# Fine-tuning a process before it becomes a skill

People arrive with a process they already run by hand. It usually works, and it
usually carries more than it needs: steps that are habit, steps that repeat a
decision every time, steps that only existed because a human was doing them.

The job is not to transcribe the process. It is to find the part that carries
the value and let the rest go.

---

## Five moves

**1. Get it written down as it happens, not as it should.** Ask them to walk
through the last time they actually did it, in order, including the parts that
felt like fumbling. The documented version of a process and the real one differ,
and the real one is what you are codifying.

**2. Find the spine.** One step in most processes carries the value, and the rest
support it. In a triage process it is the ranking. In a drafting process it is
the first pass. Name the spine out loud and check it lands. If the user disagrees,
you have found the wrong one and that is useful.

**3. Separate decisions from steps.** A decision made the same way every time is
not a step, it is a rule. Rules belong in the skill, stated once, so the model
applies them instead of re-deriving them. A decision that genuinely varies is a
branch, and branches need named conditions.

**4. Find the stopping condition.** How do they know it is done? Most people
cannot answer this on the first ask, and it matters more than it sounds: a skill
without a stopping condition pads, over-delivers, or asks one more question than
it should.

**5. Cut what a competent model would do anyway.** This is the same test the eval
step applies to assertions, and it holds here. If the model would do it without
being told, writing it down adds nothing except another way to fail.

---

## Why the cutting matters

Every step you keep is a step that can go wrong, and a skill that does six things
adequately is worse than one that does two things well.

The process a person runs by hand has steps that exist because a person was
running it. They check the calendar because they were already in it. They paste
into the doc because they were already there. None of that survives the move to a
skill, and none of it should. What survives is the spine, the rules, the branches,
and the stopping condition.

---

## What this produces

By the end of Phase 3 you should be able to say, in a few lines:

- the spine, in one sentence
- the rules the skill applies every run
- the branches, and what triggers each
- the stopping condition

If you can say those, the skill almost writes itself and the anti-patterns are
usually obvious, because each one is a way one of those four goes wrong.

If you cannot, do not move on. A skill built from a process you have not tuned
will be as long as the process and no more reliable.
